# SUNI and the EU AI Act

**This is not legal advice.** It is a description of what SUNI implements, written
so that you — or your compliance reviewer — can check the claims against the code
rather than take them on faith. Every mechanism below names the file it lives in.

Regulation (EU) 2024/1689. The **Article 50 transparency obligations became
applicable on 2 August 2026**, which is why they are implemented rather than
planned.

---

## Why this is in a self-hosted assistant at all

Most self-hosted AI assistants treat disclosure as a line of small print in the
footer. Article 50 asks for something more specific: people must know they are
talking to an AI, and synthetic output must be **marked in a machine-readable
way** — not only visibly, but detectably by other software.

That second half is the part that is easy to skip and awkward to retrofit,
because it has to happen at every point where content leaves the system. SUNI
does it in the two places where output actually escapes: generated PDFs and
outgoing email.

---

## Article 50(1) — people must know it is an AI

| Where | What |
|---|---|
| System prompt | `SUNI_SYSTEM` instructs the model to never claim to be human and to identify as an AI assistant |
| All three UIs | A persistent notice below the composer: *"SUNI is an AI assistant — responses are AI-generated and may be inaccurate."* |
| Translations | i18n key `common.ai_disclosure`, English and pt-PT |

Files: `suni/main.py`, `suni/web/ui.html`, `suni/web/chat.html`, `suni/web/face.html`

The notice is not dismissible and is not behind a settings toggle. A disclosure
the user can permanently hide is not a disclosure.

## Article 50(2) — synthetic output must be marked

**PDFs** (`suni/tools/pdf_tool.py`)

- `/Info` metadata dictionary: `Creator` set to `"SUNI (AI assistant) — artificially
  generated"`, plus `Subject` and `Keywords`. This is the machine-readable half —
  any PDF tool can read it without parsing the page.
- A visible footer on the page itself: `"AI-generated by SUNI"`.

**Email** (`suni/notifications/email_notify.py`)

- `Auto-Submitted: auto-generated` — the standard header from **RFC 3834**, which
  mail systems already understand, rather than a header invented for the purpose.
- `X-Generated-By: SUNI (AI assistant)`
- A visible disclosure in the body, in both the plain-text and HTML parts, on
  **both** send paths — a marking that only covers one code path is not a marking.

## Risk posture

SUNI is a **limited-risk** system: a general productivity assistant. Two things
follow, and both matter more than the label:

**GPAI obligations sit with the model maker, not with you.** SUNI runs stock
models (e.g. `qwen2.5`) without fine-tuning. Training-data and model-documentation
duties belong to whoever produced the model. Fine-tune a model yourself and that
changes.

**It flips to high-risk based on what you use it for, not on what it is.** Point
SUNI at an Annex III use — employment decisions, creditworthiness, and the rest —
and the high-risk regime applies to *your* deployment. Nothing in the software
detects this for you.

**Self-hosting splits the roles.** Running SUNI yourself generally makes you the
deployer, and can make you the provider if you modify it or put it on the market.
The obligations follow the roles, so a self-hosted deployment carries duties a
SaaS user would not have. This is the trade-off that comes with the control.

## Existing controls mapped to high-risk themes

SUNI is not a high-risk system and has not been through a conformity assessment.
These are noted because the foundations happen to align with the themes, which
shortens the work if a deployment ever does fall under Annex III:

| Control | File | Theme |
|---|---|---|
| Append-only audit log | `memory/audit.db`, `suni/audit.py` | Art 12 — record-keeping |
| In-chat approval gate (Allow / Deny before consequential tool calls) | `suni/approval.py` | Art 14 — human oversight |
| RBAC, shell denylist, `UNTRUSTED` content markers | `suni/rbac.py`, `suni/policy.py` | Art 15 — robustness |

## What this does not claim

- **Not a conformity assessment.** No notified body has looked at this.
- **Not a compliance guarantee for your deployment.** What you do with SUNI
  determines most of your obligations, and the software cannot know that.
- **Not GDPR coverage.** A known gap: memory supersession is a reversible
  soft-deprecation, and an erasure request needs a hard delete. Reversible removal
  is the right default for an assistant's memory and the wrong answer for Article
  17. If you process personal data, handle erasure explicitly.
- **Not a substitute for advice** from someone qualified to give it.

Corrections are welcome — open an issue. Getting this wrong in either direction
helps nobody.
